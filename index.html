01 · The data revolution

Diagnosis is getting cheaper, faster, and far more personal

The cost of generating personalized diagnostics is falling faster than Moore’s law. Genome sequencing, multi-omic profiling, and a new generation of early-detection tests are converging with continuous data from wearables and electronic health records — giving every patient a far richer picture of their own health.

Genomic sequencing

Whole-genome and multi-omic sequencing costs have collapsed, making detailed genetic profiles a realistic part of routine care.

Early-detection diagnostics

New blood-based and imaging diagnostics are entering the market, surfacing health signals years before symptoms appear.

Wearable biosignals

Continuous monitoring of heart rate, glucose, sleep, and activity turns health into a stream of data rather than a yearly checkup.

Unified health records

Interoperable EHR data means a patient’s history, labs, and prescriptions can finally sit alongside their genomic and wearable data.

02 · The therapy revolution

From one-size-fits-all to n-of-1

At the same time, the cost of designing and delivering genetic therapies for a single patient is falling. Delivery vectors like mRNA make it possible to design a treatment around one person’s biology — and regulators, including the FDA, have signaled growing openness to letting patients access these approaches.

Put together, abundant data and abundant intelligence help patients understand their real disease risk — and open the door to treatments for serious illnesses that were previously out of reach.

  1. 1

    Identify the target

    Genomic and diagnostic data point to the specific mutation or pathway driving a patient’s condition.

  2. 2

    Design the therapy

    A treatment, such as an mRNA-based therapeutic, is designed around that individual’s biology.

  3. 3

    Manufacture and deliver

    Faster, more flexible manufacturing platforms shrink the time from design to delivery.

  4. 4

    Monitor and adapt

    Ongoing data from wearables and follow-up diagnostics informs whether the approach is working — and what comes next.

How it works

An agent harness for every patient

The same kind of agent harness that powers tools like Claude Code can be pointed at a person’s health data — reading across diagnostics, genome scans, EHR records, and wearable streams to produce guidance specific to them.

Step 1

Collect

Diagnostics, genome scans, EHR records, and wearable data are gathered into one place.

Step 2

Analyze

An agent reasons across the full dataset, spotting patterns that a single test alone would miss.

Step 3

Recommend

Patients and clinicians receive specific, explainable suggestions linked back to the underlying evidence.

Step 4

Treat

When warranted, findings connect directly to rapidly designed, individualized therapies.
